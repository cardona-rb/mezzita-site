# What Bolivian (and LatAm) SMBs actually pay for in digital services, 2025-2026

Date of research: 2026-10-19. Method: web search only. Direct page fetches (WebFetch) failed with DNS errors, so everything below comes from search-result summaries. Anything marked **[UNVERIFIED]** is a vendor claim, an old figure, or a number I could not trace to a primary source. Most "adoption" statistics in this niche come from software vendors selling the thing they measure, so treat them as directional.

## 0. Macro context that changes demand
- IMF projects Bolivia as the only economy in the region to contract in 2026 and 2027; 2025 GDP -1.58%, inflation above 20% (Infobae, 2026-10-14: https://www.infobae.com/america/america-latina/2026/10/14/el-fmi-preve-que-bolivia-sera-la-unica-economia-de-la-region-que-se-contraera-en-2026-y-2027/). Dollar scarcity since Feb 2023 and fuel shortages squeeze SMB margins (opinion column, Correo del Alba, 2026-09-29; no hard SMB sales data found).
- Tourism: May-June 2026 blockades caused up to 65-90% hotel cancellations and USD 110-200M losses (El Deber: https://eldeber.com.bo/economia/bloqueos-golpean-turismo-hoteles-registran-90-cancelaciones-perdidas-superan-us-200-millones_1781318488). La Paz occupancy 32.5% in July 2026 vs 40% a year earlier (Opinion: https://www.opinion.com.bo/articulo/pais/ocupacion-hotelera-paz-alcanza-325-sector-percibe-reactivacion-gradual/20260803182900994276.html). Tarija had 85-95% occupancy on end-2025 long weekends (search summary; Tarija-specific 2026 crisis data not found). Bolivia received 1,145,165 foreign tourists in 2025 (RTVU, 2026-08-09).
- Implication: price-sensitive buyers, strong appetite for anything that cuts costs (commissions) or recovers lost revenue (no-shows, direct bookings), weak appetite for "brand" spend.

## 1. WhatsApp for commerce
- Bolivia consumer WhatsApp penetration ~75-80% (Aurora Inbox/Mazkara, 2026 **[UNVERIFIED, vendor]**). No Bolivian or Paraguayan SMB-sales-via-WhatsApp statistic found.
- Share of businesses selling via WhatsApp: Colombia 74%, Ecuador 68%, Peru 66% (Aurora Inbox, Mar 2026: https://www.aurorainbox.com/en/2026/03/05/whatsapp-business-latam-adoption/ **[UNVERIFIED, vendor, metric definitions differ]**). Regional: micro-business adoption of WhatsApp Business 12% -> 28% (2023-2025); small businesses 22% -> 45% (same vendor).
- Meta (earnings, via secondary summary): click-to-WhatsApp ad revenue +60% YoY in Q3 2025; paid messaging passed USD 2B annual run rate in Q4 2025. Global, not LatAm. Click-to-WhatsApp cost per conversation USD 1-3 in LatAm **[UNVERIFIED, blog]**. WhatsApp began showing in-app ads June 2025.
- Takeaway: WhatsApp-as-storefront is real and the default in LatAm; the Bolivia-specific numbers are missing, so say "most local businesses already sell through WhatsApp" qualitatively, not with a % claim.

## 2. QR payments in Bolivia (strongest evidence in this report)
- 2025: 891 million QR transactions worth USD 51,293M, vs USD 22,547M in 2024 (+131%); 94% of interbank electronic transfer orders; 88.4% of QR payments under Bs 500 and 49.7% under Bs 50 (Asoban data via ABI/Vision360/Red Uno, Feb 2026: https://abi.bo/transacciones-por-qr-mueve-mas-de-us-51-mil-millones-y-se-consolida-como-principal-metodo-de-pago/ ; https://www.vision360.bo/noticias/2026/02/13/39922-el-qr-se-afianza-como-principal-medio-de-pago-movio-us-51000-millones-en-2025). Note: transaction value may include transfers between individuals, not only merchant payments.
- Cash share of payments fell from 85% (2020) to 64% (2025), per BCB via El Deber (https://eldeber.com.bo/economia/billete-celular-pagos-digitales-redibujan-economia-boliviana_1779905443).
- Cards: 6.9M cards in 2023 (from 5.1M in 2019), ~46,000 POS (ASFI, 2025 PDF). 68% have an account, but only 12.6% a credit card and 44.2% a debit card (El Deber, Apr 2025). Online card purchases were restricted and only re-enabled by ASFI rules in Feb 2026 (USD 500/month floor).
- Conclusion: "pay by QR inside the chat" matches how Bolivians pay; "pay by card inside the chat" does not match the credit-card base. QR first, card as secondary option.
- CUCU: markets a QR API with a one-time integration fee of Bs 1,200 and no % commission; also SIAT e-invoicing API (https://cucu.bo/qr, https://docs.cucu.bo). **[UNVERIFIED]**: self-reported "no commission" and the fintech's ASFI authorization status (Decreto 5384 requires it).
- Ticket-size note: the typical QR payment is under Bs 50. A dental deposit or hotel deposit is a bigger, rarer payment; a restaurant order fits the pattern.

## 3. Delivery platforms
- PedidosYa Bolivia partner page shows a promo of 10% commission for 30 days plus free Plus for 60 days; regular post-promo Bolivian rate not published (https://socios.pedidosya.com.bo/es). Fees "vary by agreement". Third-party ranges of 15-30% are mostly Argentine **[UNVERIFIED for Bolivia]**. A Bolivian menu-software competitor claims apps take 25-30% **[UNVERIFIED, competitor]**. Argentina 2020: 20-43% across PedidosYa/Rappi/Glovo, triggering complaints (iProUP).
- Market (Economy, Nov 2024: https://www.economy.com.bo/articulo/business/mueve-tablero-delivery-sector-hace-mas-competitivo/20241107092450015834.html): PedidosYa launched subscriptions; EGX Turbo and Yango Food entered; Yummy exited; restaurants remain the top vertical. Yaigo (Santa Cruz origin, in Tarija since July 2019, ~500 affiliated stores per its own materials, Hugo/Yummy ownership link reported) - commissions not found. No market-share figures or Bolivian restaurant complaint coverage found.
- Takeaway: the "avoid 25-30% commission" pitch is plausible but the number is not verified for Bolivia. Ask the prospect their actual PedidosYa/Yaigo rate and use theirs. Own ordering captures repeat customers; it does not create demand for first-time customers, which apps do.

## 4. Hotels
- Booking.com commission typically 10-20% (avg ~15%), up to 25-30% with visibility programs; Bolivia-specific rate not found (PXSOL, WuBook, Reservas Directas blogs). A 2025 vendor claim that OTA share fell 30% -> 22% while direct reached 15% **[UNVERIFIED, vendor]**.
- Bolivian small-hotel spending on direct-booking tools: no data found. Tours operators and hotels depend heavily on Booking/WhatsApp/Instagram, and 2026 blockades produced cancellations, making deposit/cancellation-policy tooling relevant.
- Caution: deposits via Bolivian QR work for local guests; foreign tourists (the OTA customers) pay by foreign card, which the local QR rail does not serve. Direct booking for foreigners needs an international processor or bank transfer.

## 5. Dentists and clinics
- Doctoralia Peru pro plans: S/ 199.17 / 249.17 / 299.17 per month billed annually; website add-on S/ 55/month (https://pro.doctoralia.pe/precios). Colombia: ~COP 249,000-329,000/month per specialist (blog, Sep 2026 **[UNVERIFIED]**). No Bolivia pricing found; Doctoralia presence in Bolivia not confirmed. These are about USD 55-80/month, so the market anchor for "agenda + visibility" is real and in the same range as a 650 Bs plan.
- No-show claims: baseline 15-25% (some Mexican blogs say 25-40%); WhatsApp reminders reduce by 30-50% (many vendor blogs, e.g. https://aurorix.cl/como-reducir-no-shows-clinica-dental-chile/, https://dentalcore.app/blog/como-reducir-ausentismo-clinica-dental). **[UNVERIFIED]**: no peer-reviewed or independent source; one cited study attributes 32.6% of absences to forgetting.
- Takeaway: reminders + confirmation is the proven, cheap hook. Deposit-to-book is a stronger intervention but a friction risk; no evidence found that Bolivian patients accept it.

## 6. Law and accounting firms
- Evidence of demand is thin. Found only agency blogs (Spain/Mexico/Peru) saying GBP and websites matter for firms (noergia.com, seodw.lat, mhaconsulting.mx), plus warnings about legal-advertising restrictions that vary by country. No Bolivian data or willingness-to-pay. **[Demand: unverified.]** Accountants have a distinct driver in Bolivia: SIAT e-invoicing compliance (see below), which makes them service providers to other SMBs rather than buyers of marketing.
- SIAT: online invoicing is mandatory for groups 9-12 from 1 Oct 2026 (RND 102600000007, https://www.impuestos.gob.bo; extended from earlier deadline). A claim of all-electronic from 1 Jan 2027 is from a commercial blog and is **[UNVERIFIED]**. This is a genuine compliance-driven demand spike right now.

## 7. What SMBs pay agencies for; price points
- No reliable ranking for Bolivia. In LatAm blog consensus (agency-published, so biased) the purchase order is: social media management > website > paid ads management > local SEO/GBP > reputation > chatbots. Treat as **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- Bolivia web: one-time sites Bs 1,649-5,490 (Tecnoweb, old page); Bs 2,450-7,550 (KOM 2026: https://preciospaginaweb.com/bolivia-bolivianos-bob/); from ~USD 300 (Neolo 2026: https://www.neolo.com/blog/precio-pagina-web-bolivia.php). Hosting Bs 4-180/month. No published monthly maintenance retainers.
- Bolivia agency: Onza Marketing quotes Bs 2,766-6,455 per project/month range (https://onzamarketing.bo/); Social Media Marketing Bolivia does not publish prices.
- Peru: social media retainer S/ 1,500-4,000/month typical, SME retainers S/ 2,500-6,000; ads management S/ 850-3,500 (Funnel.pe, OnFleek, Limarank 2026). Colombia: COP 750k-1M basic, 1.15-1.5M mid, up to 3.5M premium; cheap tool-based plans from COP 279-290k (agency blogs 2026).
- Plausibility of 650 Bs/month (~USD 94 at the official 6.96 rate; the parallel rate is higher and not verified here): it sits far below Peru/Colombia agency retainers (roughly USD 400-1,000+ in Peru, USD 190-380 in Colombia basic/mid at rough conversion **[UNVERIFIED FX]**), and above chatbot subscriptions (USD 15-35/month). So 650 Bs is plausible if it bundles several managed items; it is hard to justify for one item. The 1,300 Bs setup is in line with a basic website (Bs 1,649-3,200).

## 10. AI chat/voice agents
- Linux Foundation (Dec 2025): 47% of LatAm companies implementing AI, 37% experimenting; 38% cite cost as major barrier; IDC puts it at 65%. A Mexico AWS report: chatbots 69% adoption but 72% basic use. Vendor claims such as "75% of companies with 10+ staff use conversational AI on WhatsApp" are **[UNVERIFIED]**. No voice-agent SMB adoption data for Bolivia. Verdict: real for FAQ/lead capture, hype for autonomous agents; low willingness to pay for SMBs.

## Implications for the five demos

| Demo | Demand | Rationale / emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant orders | **High** | QR rail fits small tickets (49.7% of QR payments <Bs 50); delivery-app commission pain (rate unverified, ask the prospect); WhatsApp is existing behavior. Emphasize: order summary on WhatsApp, QR pay, saved customer list for repeat orders, and a commission calculator using the owner's own PedidosYa rate. |
| Wine shop catalog + shipping | **Medium** | Catalog and QR fit; tax/alcohol-shipping and courier logistics add friction, and I found no wine-specific demand data. Emphasize: tourist cellar-door and pick-up orders, gift/bundle catalog, inventory visibility, simple delivery zones. |
| Dental booking with deposit | **Medium** (reminders: High; deposit: Low-Medium) | No-show pain is credible (15-25%+ baseline, vendor-reported) and Doctoralia price anchors (USD 55-80/mo) are similar. Deposit requires patient acceptance, and typical QR payments are small. Emphasize: WhatsApp reminders + one-tap confirm first, optional deposit only for long/expensive procedures. |
| Hotel direct booking with deposit | **Medium** | Commission savings (OTA 10-25%) are real and the 2026 blockade cancellations show a deposit/policy problem. But foreign guests cannot use local QR, and low occupancy shifts priorities to visibility. Emphasize: WhatsApp inquiry capture, GBP, a calculator of OTA commission saved, deposit via QR for domestic guests, bank transfer/foreign card for foreigners. |
| Law firm paid consultation | **Low** | No evidence of demand in Bolivia; trust-based referral market; advertising restrictions; paid-consultation-by-QR is plausible but unproven. Emphasize: GBP + reviews (carefully), WhatsApp intake form, not payment. |

**Services the site under-sells (3):**
1. SIAT e-invoicing: compliance deadline of 1 Oct 2026 for groups 9-12 creates urgent, non-discretionary demand (CUCU API partner).
2. WhatsApp reminders and confirmations (appointments, bookings): lowest-cost, most evidenced ROI.
3. WhatsApp ordering/QR collection with owner-visible savings against delivery commissions.

**Services it over-sells (2):**
1. Autonomous AI agents/voice agents: adoption is shallow, cost is a barrier, chatbots are commoditized at USD 15-35/month.
2. Card payments in chat, and NFC/QR review plates plus digital loyalty cards as headline items: card base is small (12.6% credit); review plates and loyalty have no Bolivian demand evidence found **[UNVERIFIED]**. Position them as free add-ons.
